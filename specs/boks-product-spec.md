# boks Product Spec

## Purpose

boks tests whether readers want a dedicated literature feed for complete short stories or book fragments that fit inside a 15 minute break. The prototype must support three jobs:

- readers discover, read, react, comment, share, and optionally keep a personal reading profile;
- authors submit a bok and track its review status;
- the founder reviews behavioral metrics from real test sessions, controls admin access, and moderates submitted boks.

## Current Prototype Scope

- Mobile-first responsive web app for MacBook, iPhone, and Android browsers.
- Dedicated routes: `/` for the swipe-first reader, `/author` for publishing, `/admin` for business analysis.
- The reader has two sections, feed (`/`) and series (`/series`). Every series piece is also in the feed; its production opens from the feed for every reader, not only from the shelf.
- `/series` is the boks series shelf: the pieces produced in the new formats (cinema, immersive poetry), reached from a second tab beside the feed. It is the one deliberate exception to "no shelves": only part of the catalogue will ever be produced this way, so a reader who wants these needs a place to find them rather than waiting for the ranker. The feed itself stays algorithmic and unchanged, and still offers series pieces like any other bok (marked with a small "boks series" chip that opens the production). Reads opened from the shelf carry `entry: "series"` in event metadata so algorithm comparisons can exclude them.
- Reader focus mode: the full interface appears briefly on a new story, then fades during reading so the story owns the screen. Tapping/clicking the reading surface reveals reactions and comments. There is no manual finished button; completion is inferred from time and scroll progress when the reader leaves a story.
- Reader navigation is reversible and never wraps around. **A swipe from right to left opens the next bok, and a swipe from left to right opens the previous one** - the page follows the finger, as in every paged reader and story feed. A trackpad does the same when two fingers move the same way. On a computer, a click at the right edge opens the next bok and at the left edge the previous one. (This line used to read "a tap or deliberate movement toward the left opens the previous bok", which joined two different gestures: a tap on the left edge, where "previous" is right, and a finger moving left, where it is backwards. It was implemented literally and every phone swipe went the wrong way.) On a phone, tapping the text shows or hides the controls and never changes bok. Vertical reading stays inside the current text. Reaching an edge only finishes the current scroll: changing boks requires a separate, deliberate pull that starts at the top or bottom, crosses the visible progress-ring threshold, and is released. Trackpad momentum that merely reaches the boundary must not advance the feed.
- A story change responds immediately and then settles for 480 ms along the gesture axis: horizontal navigation enters from the corresponding side, while a downward reading gesture brings the next bok from below and the reverse gesture brings the previous bok from above. There is no exit delay before the new title appears. The reader shell and controls remain fixed, repeated navigation is ignored until the transition settles, and `prefers-reduced-motion` makes the change immediate.
- Any open account, comment, author, or bok-menu dialog blocks background reader navigation. Arrow keys, Space, edge taps, swipes, and pointer drags act only on the open surface until it closes.
- Story titles and bodies use the locally bundled Source Serif 4 variable font with Latin and Cyrillic coverage. Interface controls stay in the system sans-serif. The complete SIL Open Font License 1.1 must remain reachable from `Settings -> About -> Open source licenses` and shipped with the application.
- All interface text is English. Only the content - boks, titles, bylines, comments - may be in another language.
- Appearance: System (the default, follows the device), Light or Dark, chosen in Settings (and at the foot of a guest's sign-in panel), remembered per device in localStorage and applied before first paint so a dark reader never sees a white flash. The palette is `:root[data-theme="dark"]` in `app/globals.css`; `components/boks/theme.ts` owns the rule.
- The account/settings panel closes with Escape whenever it offers a close button.
- In product language, a **bok** (plural **boks**) is one short literature unit, and **boks** is also the product's name; `CONTEXT.md` is the glossary. In Supabase storage the table is still named `reels`, and browser code still calls a bok a `Story` (`docs/adr/0007-keep-the-reels-table-name.md`).
- Supabase is the only backend: anonymous reader entry, passwordless email accounts, accounts with a unique `@handle`, published boks, signed-in likes, handle-attributed comments, attributed reader events, and admin aggregate metrics.
- Every permanent user can submit boks from `/author`, reachable from the boks wordmark on the reader surface ("Write a bok") as well as directly. New submissions are private with `pending_review` status until an admin approves them. Rejected boks stay visible to the author with a refusal reason and can be revised into a new submission.
- A bok's byline is a pen name chosen when publishing, not typed into a free "author" field on every piece and never another person's identity: the cabinet offers the byline the author used last time, falling back to their `@handle` for a first piece (`docs/adr/0005-handle-is-identity-byline-is-pen-name.md`).
- Signing up asks for an email and nothing else. The `@handle` is derived from the local part of the address; if it is taken, a random number is appended. It can be changed later in settings, and it is the only name a *person* has - there is no separate display name.
- The byline on a bok belongs to that piece, not to the account. Changing your handle therefore moves your `@handle` everywhere but never rewrites work you already published - a pen name is a decision about a text, not a stale copy of a profile.
- A person's identity - the `@handle` - is stored once, in `user_profiles`, and joined wherever it is shown, so changing it changes it on every comment with no backfill. The byline (`reels.author_name`) is the one name stored per bok, by design. Imported catalogue boks carry their own byline under a publisher account.
- `boks_public_identity` is the one published projection of a person: the `@handle`, which already appears on every comment. Everything else on a profile - role, terms, preferences - stays behind row-level security.
- A byline reads as a name ("Mira Vale"), not a username, which is why it lives on the bok. Everywhere a person is identified - comments, admin, the People list, the account panel - the `@handle` is the identifier.
- A submission is a title and a text. The title is optional: left empty, it becomes the text's first line with words on it (a `* * *` divider or a lone dash is skipped, a trailing comma or dash is dropped, and a line past 80 characters is cut at a word and marked with `…`). The rule is `boks_title_from_text` in the database, applied by a trigger on every write, and mirrored by `lib/boks/bok-title.ts` so the form can show the title the author is about to get. There is no opening hook: it was retired on 2026-09-19 and `reels.hook` is kept empty.
- An author may submit at most 3 boks per UTC day. The limit is enforced in the database by `submit_bok_for_review` and reported by `get_my_bok_quota`, so the author cabinet shows the remaining allowance and the reset time before anything is written. The limit exists to keep the review queue readable, not to rate-limit reading.
- Guest readers start immediately with no visible sign-up wall. After 5 distinct viewed boks they see a gentle notice that says they can read 10 more **without an account**; after 10 it says 5 more without an account; after 15 they must sign up or sign in to continue. These messages must say that reading is free and explain that an account saves history and improves recommendations. Never describe the remaining allowance as "free boks" because all reading is free.
- Guest readers can open and read the complete comment conversation, but writing, replying, and liking comments require a free account. Comments use one-level threads: a reply belongs to a root comment on the same bok, and replies cannot nest again. A commenter is identified by their unique `@handle` alone. The comment sheet opens at reading-friendly partial height and can expand to the full reader surface by its handle or explicit control. The sign-up copy should explain that accounts improve recommendations and keep comments tied to the reader's profile.
- Sign-up and sign-in are passwordless: the reader enters an email and receives one message carrying both a one-time link and a 6-digit code; either signs them in (`docs/adr/0006-passwordless-email-sign-in.md`). The code exists for boks opened from an iPhone home screen, which a link from the mail app cannot reach. No boks password exists. Sign-up asks for email and Terms acceptance only; the `@handle` is derived after authentication and can be changed later in settings.
- New accounts must accept the current Terms before creation. The profile stores the accepted terms version and timestamp. The Terms include the author content license needed for boks to host, recommend, distribute, promote, demo, and advertise submitted works while authors retain ownership.
- When the reading chrome is visible, every reader has a dedicated 44 x 44 account/settings button at the top left. Signed-in readers use it to manage identity settings and search separate Read, Liked, and Commented views of their account library. Opening an item places that one bok at the reader's current position; the next gesture continues through the recommendation feed rather than a library queue. These views never appear for guests. The centered boks wordmark is branding, not an undiscoverable settings target.
- A reader can follow an author, and following is a durable relationship that requires a durable identity: guests are asked to create an account rather than silently failing. Following raises that author in the feed and counts as standing positive evidence in the reader profile.
- While reading, the follow control sits beside the byline and shows the relationship rather than only the action: once followed it recedes into a quiet checked state instead of staying a filled button. The account panel lists everyone the reader follows, with a one-tap unfollow, and the two stay in step.
- Responses are a conversation about one piece, so they read chronologically, each carries who said it and how long ago, and a reply from the author of the bok is marked as such. The sheet uses the height it has; the composer is one row within thumb reach, and Enter sends.
- The byline is the way to the author: tapping it opens everything they have published, with a follow control and the bok being read marked so the reader keeps their place. An author is addressed by @handle, so the seeded catalog - which has a byline but no account - can still be opened and read; it simply cannot be followed, and says so.
- Every author has their own address, `/a/<handle>`, and every bok has `/b/<id>`; the share action produces the latter. Sharing the feed URL would open a different bok for whoever received it.
- Reader accounts must not expose genre, mood, category, or algorithm preference controls. The feed learns from behavior instead of asking the reader to configure what they want.
- The reading surface shows no metadata about the piece: no reading-time estimate, no genre or mood tags, no category labels. Only the wordmark, the title, the text, the author, and the way forward. A minute counter makes the reader think about time instead of content, and a "speculative · strange" label asks them to classify a piece before they have read it. Genre, mood and length are still collected from the author and still drive ranking - they are inputs to the feed, never furniture around the story.
- Admin access is a database-backed role on `user_profiles`. Admins can grant or remove admin status only from `/admin`; the only exception is first-admin bootstrap when no admin exists yet.
- Admin role changes are database-checked, visible immediately in People, and protect the acting admin and the last active admin from accidental demotion. A suspended account must be restored before it can become an admin; an admin must be demoted before it can be suspended or deleted.
- Admins may suspend a permanent account with a reason. Suspended readers may continue reading but cannot like, comment, follow, report, or publish. Restoring the account re-enables those actions without losing its history.
- Admin account deletion is irreversible and requires the exact `@handle`. The Auth user, profile, reactions, and comments are deleted; authored boks are anonymised and moved to Deleted instead of being erased. A UUID-only tombstone blocks already-issued JWTs without retaining email or profile data.
- Admins review recently submitted boks in `/admin`, approve suitable boks into the reader feed, or refuse them with a clear reason shown in the author cabinet.
- Removing a published bok is a two-step lifecycle. `Delete` first archives it, removes it from recommendations, and records who removed it, when, and why. Deleted boks remain searchable in `Content -> Deleted`, where an admin may restore a rights-confirmed piece or permanently delete it after typing its exact title. Permanent deletion cascades to its reactions, comments, reports, and metrics.
- Supabase is the only data store. The original D1 prototype backend was removed on 2026-10-05 (`docs/adr/0013-remove-d1-compatibility-path.md`).
- A curated public-domain catalogue imported under a publisher account, plus admin-approved boks submitted by authors.
- The product ranker is `for_you`, ported from the original boks backend: retrieval families (resume / affinity / discovery), a reader profile built from completions, likes, comments, dwell and repeated non-attention, author and freshness and cold-start priors, and a greedy slate that spaces authors, shapes and lengths. It explains every placement it makes, and those explanations are stored with the served slate. It is the control arm and, while the audience is small, the only live arm, so any other algorithm has to beat what readers actually get (`docs/adr/0008-for-you-is-control-single-live-arm.md`).
- The form of a piece (verse / short prose / long prose) is derived from its text, never declared by its author, and feeds the ranking. Genre and mood remain author-declared inputs to ranking and are never shown to the reader.
- One recommendation implementation (`lib/boks/recommendations`) ranks every feed. The Worker's `/api/feed` is the canonical path and records the served slate; the browser runs the same code only as a labelled fallback. The database returns candidates with behaviour counters; scoring and ordering happen in TypeScript, so adding an algorithm is one file and needs no SQL.
- Attributed reader events on Supabase: every impression, start, completion, skip, like, share, comment, heartbeat and aftertaste carries its algorithm, experiment, segment, feed rank, elapsed time and estimated duration. Guest readers are measured too.
- The live traffic split between algorithms is database-backed and editable from `/admin`, so comparing a new strategy does not need a deploy. Assignment stays deterministic per reader.
- Algorithm comparison is reader-level: one reader is one sample, however much they read. Each comparison reports an effect, a 95% interval, the readers behind it, and what a sample that size could have detected. An interval spanning zero is reported as inconclusive, never as "no difference". Composite scores, event-level confidence labels and per-segment breakdowns were removed for being numbers that looked like measurements and were not.
- JSON metric export for analysis or investor updates.
- Browser code uses only `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`; service role keys are never used in frontend code.

## Validation Questions

- Do readers start stories when the promise is under 15 minutes?
- Do they complete after starting?
- Do they react or comment after completion?
- Which story attributes correlate with starts, completions, rereads, and reactions?
- Do authors understand the 15 minute constraint and submit suitable pieces?
- Do authors understand review status, rejection reasons, and how to revise a refused bok?
- Does a 3-per-day submission limit feel reasonable to a serious author, or does it block real work?
- Does hiding manual choice improve story starts compared with chooser-based browsing?

## MVP Extension Points

- Authentication: keep Supabase permanent users as the identity source and keep the user-facing flow passwordless (email link or code). Passkeys can be considered later if email creates friction.
- Authorization: keep admin role mutation behind Supabase RPC checks; never trust browser metadata or local storage for admin decisions.
- Recommendation: add strategies to the registry, then add precomputed daily rollups when live aggregation gets slow, then reader history and story embeddings.
- Content safety: add moderation before public publishing.
- Growth loops: shared bok pages (`/b/<id>`) and author pages (`/a/<handle>`) with server-rendered previews exist; next are richer referral attribution and invite flows.
- Monetization: add author subscriptions, paid collections, or sponsor-supported free reading.
- Operations: add pagination, moderation queues, cohort exports, and author-level reporting once inventory reaches thousands of texts.
- Supabase analytics: attributed event and assignment tables now exist. The next step is precomputed daily rollups so the lab does not aggregate the raw event stream on every load.
